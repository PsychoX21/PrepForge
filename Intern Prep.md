**Software Engineer**

* Almost all the companies have a competitive programming test as their first test round (based on which they shortlist you for another test or interview).  
* **(\*)** One of the most fundamental websites to start CP is: [CSES](https://cses.fi/problemset/). There are roughly 400 questions in it, you do not need to do all, but complete all the initial sections. Even inside these sections, you can leave “the very difficult” questions (those which have the least number of successful  submissions). But still, you should complete around 180 of these 400 problems.  
* **(\*)** If you are a beginner to CP, you can follow my [SOC](https://github.com/sakshamrathi21/SOC-Conquering_Competitive_Programming). It has a lot of resources (topic-wise) and roughly 150 problems (with huge diversity).  
* One of the best books to read (basic) theory for CP is [steven-halim](https://github.com/sakshamrathi21/SOC-Conquering_Competitive_Programming/blob/main/Resources/Steven-Halim_-Felix-Halim-Competitive-Programming-3_-The-New-Lower-Bound-of-Programming-Contests-Lulu.com-_2013_.pdf). There are other CP handbooks, which are quite relevant to CSES problems such as [1](https://github.com/sakshamrathi21/SOC-Conquering_Competitive_Programming/blob/main/Resources/book.pdf), [2](https://github.com/sakshamrathi21/SOC-Conquering_Competitive_Programming/blob/main/Resources/guide-t-cp.pdf). You might need to revise data structures a bit, so you can use this [website](https://cp-algorithms.com) or this [doc](https://github.com/sakshamrathi21/Competitive_Programming/blob/main/resources/1757217119433.pdf)**.**  
* **(\*)** Next, you should start with [codeforces](https://codeforces.com). Apart from solving the questions for practice (this website has good solutions for most of the problems), you should also appear for contests. For most of the company tests, a rating of 1600 will suffice (provided if you are active on codeforces, if you aren’t then this metric isn’t important for you). But, some companies like Rubrik, have difficult CP tests too. The basic formula which I had adopted was, if my rating is “x”, then I used to solve problems in the rating range \[x+100, x \+ 200\] (although I couldn’t appear for many contests, because of my second year internship meets).  
* A few believe that questions on codeforces aren’t much oriented towards what companies ask in their tests (like they are a bit more focussed on math, and not much on core DSA concepts). I feel, the thought process we develop is more important (along with the speed with which we are forced to solve questions in a contest). But still, such concerns might be valid, so please check the point below.  
* Some also follow [leetcode](https://leetcode.com). Some of the questions here are frequently asked in company tests. Instead of writing the entire code, you will be asked to write just the functions (which aligns with the pattern of various company tests). This [sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1-wKcV99KtO91dXdPkwmXGTdtyxAfk1mbPXQg81R9sFE/edit?gid=0#gid=0) (and [this](https://leetcode.com/problem-list/eeudwo2i/) one) contain some relevant problems (heavily followed by users across the globe).  Another general suggestion would be to solve 10 hard-marked problems from each of the topics listed on the top (specifically from the “Design” section).  
* The overall goal should be to solve 5-10 good questions each day (depending on how much time you have). Across a period of 2.5 months, you should be able to do at least 250-300 questions. Also make sure that you increase your overall speed while solving these questions. Being able to solve a question is quite different from being able to solve it in a timed and pressurised environment.

**Resume Related Tips**

* Typically people create 4 resumes, two for software, and other two for quant roles. Both of these categories are further divided into single page and double page resumes.  
* **(\*)** Limit the sizes of some of the sections such as \- PoR, Extracurricular. In case you have many projects, then remove the “courses undertaken” section.  
* If you did/are doing any internship (research/company) or you have ever worked with a professor, then try to describe that in a separate section and add it at the top (provided it is relevant to one of the core CS sub-domains).  
* Do not add full stops anywhere in your resume. Try to remove the whitespace, after the sentence ends (try rephrasing, or adding random small spaces in between) (You can afford to have enough spacing in a **CV**). There should not be many fancy words in your projects’ descriptions, which impacts badly in general.  
* **(\*)** If you do not have enough projects, and you have time to start a few, then please do so soon. They can even be reading projects. Most of my projects can be found [here](https://github.com/sakshamrathi21).  
* Refrain from adding redundant projects (like if a similar project already exists, you can merge both of these). Try to make the description more technical, backed by numbers. Also make sure that the content is not random (or contains fancy english), it is quite easy to catch false descriptions. It is a good habit to maintain github repos for each of your projects.  
* You should be well aware of whatever you have added in your resume. You can be asked anything from it, in your interviews. Some companies even do a resume filtering round (after an initial test, or maybe before it).  
* A resume repository can be found at this [link](https://drive.google.com/drive/u/1/folders/1V8fSpU4S08oGp-2KL4Y--TVdi6Hjrpb1).  
* Lastly, start creating your resumes as early as you can. Before making a final submission, make sure you send it to a few seniors, for review.

**General Tips**

* Most of the international firms demand great communication skills (especially Optiver) from their applicants. You should try to increase your English fluency. For this, just practice speaking English with your friends/family. You should also be capable of showing good body language. Some of the best books I have read are [How to Talk with Anyone](https://dn790008.ca.archive.org/0/items/HowToTalkAnyone/how%20to%20talk%20anyone.pdf) and the [one](https://www.rfpmm.org/pdf/how-to-win-friends-and-influence-people.pdf) by Dale Carnegie. Apart from this, try to increase your vocabulary space over the summer, either through reading novels/story books or maybe through watching English movies (make sure you manage your time properly).  
* You will have a long summer vacation before your third year intern season. Mostly you will be able to complete all of the important stuff from this doc in those 3 months. Just try to increase your overall productivity (this [one](https://dn790007.ca.archive.org/0/items/atomic-habits-pdfdrive/Atomic%20habits%20%28%20PDFDrive%20%29.pdf) has some tips).  
* This [website](https://github.com/northwesternfintech/2025QuantInternships) provides a list of some of the quant companies for internships, it also contains some reviews. I haven’t read this myself though.  
* A few firms have also added their mock interviews either on their career pages or on youtube. Make sure you look at them, and see what all they expect from you. One common expectation is that you should not just solve the question in your mind, and speak out the answer, you should actually walk them through your thinking process (this would give you more time too), to help the interviewer know if you are going in the right direction.  
* Please follow all these quant firms on LinkedIn. They often release important updates about their events there (prior to when they are available to the other public). If you find time, develop your LinkedIn profile, you might start receiving messages from HRs too ([mine](http://www.linkedin.com/in/sakshamrathi21) isn’t that developed still).  
* Apart from the firms which come to college, you can also try apping to various other firms (which do not come to the intern season).
